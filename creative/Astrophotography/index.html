Astrophotography

Long-exposure frames from dark skies.

Space was my first special interest. My earliest memory of it is talking to my dad about space, from a top bunk, before I was old enough to go to school. He built a roll-off roof observatory from his own design when I was a kid, built several more for other people, and it was his old smart telescope, passed on to me when he upgraded, that got me taking my own images. The underlying question that connects all of it is whether there is anyone out there. Nobody knows yet. That feels like the most important open problem there is.

The garden is surrounded on most sides by fruit farms whose polytunnels glow all night and bounce off the cloud cover. You plan around the weather, you wait, and then you go outside at eleven in the evening and look at something that has been there since before your species existed.

The raw output looks like nothing until you process it. Stacking hundreds of exposures reduces the noise until the signal underneath becomes coherent. The algorithms for doing this are not new, most of them have been sitting in image processing literature for decades. What is surprisingly rare is people being willing to actually experiment, to treat the data as something to be interrogated rather than a photo to be tidied up. It is not so different from bioinformatics. The final image is an argument you are making about what was actually there.
