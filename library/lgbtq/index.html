European Court of Human Rights / UK Parliament · 11 July 2002 / 1 July 2004

Christine Goodwin v United Kingdom [2002] ECHR 588 and the Gender Recognition Act 2004

The European Court of Human Rights grand chamber ruling establishing trans people's right to legal recognition of their gender under international human rights law, and the Gender Recognition Act 2004 to which it directly gave rise. Together they form the legal foundation for gender recognition in the United Kingdom. The protections established by these instruments have been materially compromised in the UK by the Supreme Court's 2025 ruling in For Women Scotland v Scottish Ministers.

Legal basis: sections 29, 30, and 171(3) of the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 (research, reporting of current events, public interest, and, in the opinion of the operator, documentation of UK constitutional law).

UK Parliament · 2004–2019

The Legislative History of Same-Sex Relationship Recognition in the United Kingdom

The four Acts of Parliament establishing legal recognition for same-sex relationships across the United Kingdom: the Civil Partnership Act 2004, which first provided legal recognition for same-sex couples across the UK; the Marriage (Same Sex Couples) Act 2013 (England and Wales); the Marriage and Civil Partnership (Scotland) Act 2014 (Scotland); and the Northern Ireland (Executive Formation etc) Act 2019 (Northern Ireland), which completed the process of legalising same-sex marriage across all four nations.
